# Hindi gold-data oracles

The oracle pair for the Hindi verification loop: two machine-readable,
full-paradigm sources, so their agreement is the scoring gold and their
disagreements form the adjudication corpus.

## Why this pair

1. **kaikki.org Hindi** (Wiktextract, CC BY-SA). `scripts/hin/fetch_kaikki.sh`
   → `scripts/hin/kaikki_to_tsv.py` → `data/hin/kaikki.tsv`. The
   by-part-of-speech verb extraction (~10,500 verb lemmas, each a full
   `hi-conj` inflection table). This is the linguistically careful leg:
   its perfective participles, contracted stems and light-verb forms are
   correct.
2. **UniMorph hin** (Batsuren & Cotterell; English-Wiktionary lineage,
   CC BY-SA 3.0), commit-pinned and sha256-checked.
   `scripts/hin/fetch_unimorph.sh` → `scripts/hin/unimorph_to_tsv.py` →
   `data/hin/unimorph.tsv`. A fully-generated paradigm: 258 verb lemmas ×
   211 feature bundles = 54,438 rows, with the analytic tenses spelled
   out as multi-word forms.

Both legs are ultimately Wiktionary-derived, so this is not a fully
independent cross-check the way a national lexicon crossed with kaikki
would be; it is the same pairing the Armenian language ships
(UniMorph = the validated Wiktionary leg). What the pair *does* buy is a
real adjudication signal, because the two were generated by different
pipelines and disagree in one systematic place — see **Disagreements**.

The two share **255** lemmas (UniMorph's 258 minus three the kaikki
extraction spells differently) and agree on **48,421** of 52,129 shared
slots (**92.89%**); the 3,708 disagreements are all one UniMorph defect,
adjudicated below.

## Feature schema

`lemma ⇥ form ⇥ features`, UniMorph bundles. The kaikki adapter emits the
*same* bundles so the harness can intersect them. The paradigm scored is:

**Synthetic core** (31 bundles):

- `V;NFIN;LGSPEC1` / `LGSPEC2` — the infinitive (उठना) and the oblique
  infinitive (उठने);
- `V;{1,2,3};{SG,PL};SBJV` — the subjunctive (उठूं, उठे, उठें, उठो);
- `V;{1,2,3};{SG,PL};SBJV;{MASC,FEM}` — the synthetic future in
  `-गा/-गे/-गी` (उठूंगा, उठेगी, उठेंगे);
- `V;2;SG;IMP;INFM`, `V;2;SG;IMP;FORM`, `V;2;PL;IMP;FORM` — the three
  imperatives by politeness (तू उठ, तुम उठो, आप उठिए);
- `V;V.PTCP;{MASC,FEM};{SG,PL};{IPFV,PFV}` — the imperfective and
  perfective participles (उठता/उठते/उठती, उठा/उठे/उठी).

**Analytic layer** (180 bundles): an aspect stem plus the conjugated
copula होना —
`V;{1,2,3};{SG,PL};{HAB,PRF,PROG};{PRS,PST,SBJV,LGSPEC3,LGSPEC4};{MASC,FEM}`.
Three aspects (habitual = imperfective participle, perfect = perfective
participle, progressive = `stem रहा`) each cross five copula tense-moods
(present है, past था, subjunctive हो, presumptive होगा `LGSPEC3`,
counterfactual होता `LGSPEC4`) and agree in gender and number. This is a
bounded, defensible cut of Hindi's analytic system: it is exactly the set
UniMorph spells out, and it deliberately **excludes** the further
V1+light-verb compounds (उठ जाना, कर लेना) and the future-perfect /
future-subjunctive analytics kaikki additionally lists — those are left
out of the schema so the two oracles describe the same cells.

### Orthographic normalization

Three optional-orthography axes, on which the two oracles split
systematically, are folded identically in both adapters (as the Armenian
loop folds the emphasis mark and the եւ ligature):

- **candrabindu → anusvara** (कमाऊँ → कमाऊं);
- the **optional euphonic य-glide** between a vowel and an ए/ई ending
  (कमाये → कमाए, उठिये → उठिए) — the masculine-singular ...ाया, on which
  the oracles agree, keeps its glide;
- the **optional feminine-plural nasal** (उठीं → उठी, होतीं → होती).

The subject pronoun UniMorph prefixes to the bare subjunctive and future
(`मैं उठूं`) is also stripped, since kaikki cites those forms bare.

## The engine

`src/hin.rs` drops the `-ना` to get the stem and builds the whole system
by rule: the subjunctive endings, the future as subjunctive + `-गा`, the
two participles, the three imperatives, and the analytic layer as an
aspect stem plus a hardcoded होना copula table. A vowel-final stem takes
the य-glide and independent vowel signs where a consonant stem takes bare
matras (खाया vs उठा). Compound and light-verb lemmas (`अध्ययन करना`,
`हो जाना`) conjugate only their last word and carry the rest along
unchanged, which covers UniMorph's many multiword lemmas for free.

What the rules cannot predict lives in `data/hin/verbs.tsv` (8 rows): the
suppletive and contracted stems of होना (हुआ), जाना (गया), करना (किया,
कीजिए), देना (दिया, दूं), लेना (लिया), and the ई-stem shorteners पीना
(पिया), जीना, सीना. It is regenerated by `scripts/hin/mine_verbs.py` from
`scripts/hin/manual.tsv`.

## Disagreements

All **3,708** oracle disagreements are one systematic UniMorph hin
defect: for a subset of lemmas it forms the perfective participle and the
vowel-initial subjunctive/imperative endings by splicing an *independent*
vowel onto the consonant stem — बनऊं, बनया, बनइए, बनई — where Hindi
orthography requires the matra: बनूं, बना, बनिए, बनी (and हुई/किया/की for
the suppletives, which UniMorph writes होई/किया-derived कियी). kaikki
spells every one correctly, so each disagreement is ruled **o1** (first
oracle = kaikki) in [`disagreements.tsv`](disagreements.tsv), and the
engine follows kaikki.
